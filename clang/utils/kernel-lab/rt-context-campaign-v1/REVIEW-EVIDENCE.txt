Independent phase-3 API-fixture effect/entry review, frozen before execution.
The source external-effect-definitions.c is reviewed evidence for the three
external declarations: an RT lock acquire/release may wait on PREEMPT_RT;
schedule is an ordinary sleeping operation; foreign_context_action is opaque.
These are effect capabilities, not guarantees that a call will physically sleep.
No arbitrary helper-name whitelist may determine them. Manifest fields bind exact
source file/hash/token-offset, function type and linkage, with profile macros,
target triple and every real loaded file/hash verified before any seed/effect.

An explicit task/softirq/threaded-IRQ fixture with IRQs enabled and no preemption
or raw-lock prohibition permits the declared RT wait. BH/RCU/migration context
alone does not forbid RT wait. Raw lock, preemption disabled, hard IRQs disabled,
hard IRQ and NMI fixture entries forbid RT wait on RT. A declared RT-only wait
has no RT waiting effect on non-RT. Ordinary sleeping operations remain invalid
in hard IRQ or an ordinary RCU read section. Entry facts are borrowed ambient
context; they are not locks or irqsave tokens owned by the current function.
Only top-frame analysis may install an entry seed. Inlining the same helper must
preserve the caller's actual context. Same-spelling internal functions in other
TUs must not share entry/effect identities. Unknown effect yields explicit
incomplete context; a prior known witness cannot disappear. We do not assert
post-opaque context preservation or require a new certain witness after it.

Stale source, header, config/autoconf, evidence or target identity; conflicting
records; and duplicate JSON keys must cause explicit incomplete validation and
must not authorize seeds/effects. The no-manifest control preserves existing
primitive behavior. Failed/timed-out/zero-analysis executions are coverage gaps.
The frozen oracle describes these predicates independently of compiler output.
